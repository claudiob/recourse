Look and feel

Two lines in an initializer say how every page looks. The rest is the reader's — their palette, their page size, their time zone, how much a phone spells out — and this guide is where each of those is kept, where the assets come from, and how the words are changed.

The primary color

# config/initializers/recourse.rb
Recourse.color = :orange

Which Bootstrap family every button, link, sorted heading and focus ring is drawn in: one of blue, gray, orange, purple, pink or brown, or nil for Bootstrap's own blue. Anything else raises, naming the six, since a typo would otherwise go unnoticed until somebody looked at a button. The label on a solid button is white or dark by family, so orange gets dark text rather than white on it.

The theme

Recourse.theme = :nord

Which color scheme the pages are drawn in — one of Recourse::THEMES.keys: dawn, dracula, gruvbox, monokai, nord, one_dark, solarized, tokyo_night, and bootstrap for upstream's own palette, which is also what nil means. A palette repaints Bootstrap's ramps in both modes and leads with an accent of its own, so a theme alone looks right; a color named beside it still says which family is primary. Every page follows the system's light or dark setting until the reader says otherwise.

What the reader chooses

The theme is where a reader starts, not where they stay. The moon at the foot of the sidebar — a sun while the page is dark — moves the page to another palette and into the other mode, and the choice is kept in their browser, never on the server. Two more choices are kept as cookies, since the server reads them back: the page size, 15 or 100 rows, offered under every table; and on a phone, whether the chrome is icons alone or icons with their words, toggled by the arrows at the foot of the sidebar.

The reader's time zone

Every time on these pages is drawn in the reader's own zone. The browser reports it into a cookie, and the server wraps each action in Time.use_zone of it, so the page, the table, the calendar and the field that edits a value all agree. A browser that has not said, or names a zone Rails does not know, gets the host's config.time_zone. The host's own setting is never written and its own screens are drawn as before; storage stays UTC.

Where the assets come from

Everything a page is styled and scripted by — Bootstrap 6, its icons, Turbo, Stimulus and the controllers this markup names — is bh, a dependency of the gem, served by its own engine at /bh/. Nothing is vendored here and nothing is fetched across a network, and the version in the lock file is the version on the page.

A host serving those files from somewhere else — a CDN, a bundle of its own carrying bh's layer — says so, with no trailing slash, and that is the whole of what it writes:

Recourse.assets = 'https://cdn.example.com/bh'

The host's head and layout

The head past the gem's own tags is the host's. Whatever a recourses/head partial holds — the icon the tab wears, a stylesheet of the host's own over this one — is drawn last in the head, from the host's own views or from a gem it loads, and the gem writes no icon link of its own where nobody has:

# app/views/recourses/_head.html.erb
<link rel='icon' href='/favicon.svg' type='image/svg+xml'>

Every screen renders in the gem's layouts/recourses, never in the host's application layout. A host puts its own chrome back by writing app/views/layouts/recourses.html.erb, which wins on being earlier in the view paths, or by declaring layout 'application' on RecoursesController. Such a layout yields :actions and :search where it wants the buttons and the search box, and gets no styling until it links the stylesheet.

The locale

Every string the gem shows is a key in config/locales/recourse.en.yml, and a host rewords one by writing the key in a locale of its own: recourse.add is Add %{model}, recourse.none is No %{models}., recourse.deletion.undone is This cannot be undone. Dates and times read from date.formats.recourse and time.formats.recourse, namespaced so the host's own formats stand, and a locale without them raises rather than degrading. A column heading is renamed by translating the attribute, and a model's deletion is worded under recourse.models; see Models.
